# Axiomara Overview

Most planning tools store lists. Axiomara is built to reason across the relationships between those lists: what you want, what is scheduled, what changed, what your current capacity looks like, and which next move has the strongest evidence.

## From records to decisions

Axiomara begins with four human-readable records:

| Record | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Task** | A concrete action that can be completed. |
| **Goal** | A desired outcome that may require several actions. |
| **Daily Rhythm** | A repeatable behavior that supports consistency. |
| **Note** | Context, research, reflection, or an idea that should not be forced into a commitment. |

Those records become more useful when the system can connect them. A goal can provide direction to a task. A note can explain a constraint. A Daily Rhythm can support a goal. Timeline can place action in time without becoming a second authoring system.

## Three operating signals

- **Energy** represents available capacity inferred from the evidence you provide. It is not a medical measurement.
- **Clarity** represents how well the next decision is supported and understood.
- **Momentum** represents sustained evidence of meaningful follow-through over time.

These signals should change when their supporting evidence changes. They are planning aids, not scores of personal worth.

## Three levels of intelligence

1. **Orientation:** Nexus summarizes the current operating state and highlights what deserves attention.
2. **Planning:** Smart Planner turns selected records and constraints into a feasible, explainable plan.
3. **Investigation:** SI Console examines a broader question, compares trade-offs, and exposes assumptions, limitations, and possible actions.

Trajectory Engine extends the view forward. Progression looks backward at recorded evidence. Together, they create a feedback loop rather than a one-time answer.

## What makes an Axiomara answer useful

Every consequential recommendation should make five things understandable:

- **Context:** which user-approved records and signals influenced the response;
- **Reasoning:** why the recommendation follows from that context;
- **Assumptions:** what the system inferred or could not verify;
- **Action:** what the user could do next;
- **Control:** what will happen only if the user chooses it.

## Product boundaries

Axiomara can organize information, support planning, and explain possible actions. It cannot know every part of a person's life, guarantee a future outcome, or replace professional judgment. The user decides whether a recommendation fits.
