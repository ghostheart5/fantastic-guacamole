# Goals

> Guide updated October 23, 2026. Screen names can vary by installed version. The October 22–23 repairs are tested and merged; this does not establish that a new Google Play build is available. See [[Release Notes|Release-Notes]].

Goals are broader outcomes worth working toward. They give your actions direction without requiring you to know every step before you begin.

Use a goal when you can describe a meaningful result, not just the next thing to do.

## Create a Goal in Add something

Goals are a Add something-owned input type. In [[Add something|Creator]], choose **Goal**, give it a clear outcome-focused title, and save the smallest useful version.

Examples:

- Complete a professional certification
- Improve physical health
- Publish a portfolio website
- Save for a planned expense
- Learn a new skill

You can refine a goal later. Starting with a clear direction is more useful than waiting for a perfect plan.

## Goals, tasks, habits, and notes

Each input type serves a different purpose.

| Type | Use it for | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Goal** | A broader outcome | Complete a professional certification |
| **Task** | One concrete action | Choose a course |
| **habit** | A repeatable action | Study for 20 minutes each weekday |
| **Note** | Context or information | Questions to research before enrolling |

Goals can be supported by tasks, habits, and notes, but they do not need all three. Add only the support that helps you move forward.

## Turn direction into a next action

After you create a goal, ask one practical question:

> What is the smallest useful action I can take next?

For example:

```text
Goal: Publish a portfolio website
Next task: Choose one project to feature
Supporting note: Collect examples and links
habit: Spend 20 minutes improving the portfolio on weekdays
```

The goal provides direction. The next action gives you something you can actually do now.

## Schedule and review in Schedule

Use [[Schedule|Timeline]] when a supporting action benefits from a place in time. Schedule helps you plan, review, and execute scheduled action; it does not replace Add something as the place where goals and their supporting inputs are authored.

Avoid turning every goal into a crowded schedule. Schedule the actions that need timing, then review and adjust as reality changes.

## Use goals across Axiomara

- [[Today|Nexus]] can use the goals and other inputs in your system as context for your current operating state and next-best action.
- [[Chat|Smart Planner]] can provide explainable planning assistance when you want help reconciling available inputs into a feasible plan.
- [[SI Chat|SI Console]] can consider relevant goal context for deeper strategic investigation and executable guidance.
- Predictions can examine future scenarios, assumptions, and corrections using broader system context.
- Activity focuses on evidence-backed advancement and leverage actions.

These surfaces are optional and do not guarantee a recommendation, a schedule, measurable progress, or completion. Review any guidance before acting and decide what fits your situation.

## Write useful goals

Useful goals are clear enough to guide a next action and flexible enough to adapt.

| Less useful | More useful |
| --- | --- |
| Be better at business | Launch a small freelance service |
| Get healthier | Build a sustainable walking routine |
| Learn more | Complete an introductory course in a topic I care about |

You do not need to force a deadline, percentage, or detailed hierarchy into every goal. Add detail when it helps you make a better decision or take a useful next step.

## Review and adjust

Revisit a goal when circumstances change or the next step is unclear:

- Is this still an outcome I care about?
- What evidence tells me it is moving forward—or not?
- Is there one action I can take now?
- Does the plan need to be simplified, revised, or paused?

Changing or retiring a goal can be the right decision. It is not a failure to update your direction with new information.

## What goals are not

Goals are not:

- A required parent for every task or habit
- A promise that Axiomara will automatically measure progress
- A rigid lifecycle or mandatory hierarchy
- A substitute for choosing a realistic next action

“Mission” may appear as strategic context elsewhere in Axiomara, but it is not a required Goal parent, Add something record type, or dedicated Goal screen.
